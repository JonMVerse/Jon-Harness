# Workforce Monitoring Policy (v0.1, draft)

This is a placeholder policy used by the assessment agent. HR, Legal, and
Privacy jointly own the real version; replace this file when the canonical one
is ready.

## Principle

We do not monitor employees covertly. Where AI capabilities have the side-effect
of analysing employee activity (messages, code commits, meeting transcripts,
calendar data, productivity metrics), this is workforce monitoring and is
subject to this policy.

## When AI use is workforce monitoring

A use case constitutes workforce monitoring (in whole or in part) if it:
- Analyses employee communications (Slack, email, chat) at the individual level
- Scores or rates individual employees on output, productivity, sentiment, or
  behaviour
- Records or summarises meetings without informed consent of all participants
- Tracks individuals across systems to build a profile

Aggregate analytics that cannot be re-identified to an individual are usually
not workforce monitoring (but should still be assessed).

## Required when workforce monitoring is in scope

### Transparency
- Clear notice to employees describing what is collected, why, how it is used,
  who has access, and how long it is retained.
- The notice must precede any data collection, not be retrofitted.

### Consultation
- Where works councils exist (EU/EEA) — formal consultation as required by
  national law.
- Where union recognition exists (UK or elsewhere) — engagement with the
  recognised representative body.
- Even where no formal consultation is required, line manager + HR engagement
  on the design choices is expected.

### Lawful basis
- Legitimate interest is rarely sufficient on its own for monitoring at work.
- Article 88 GDPR-derived national rules apply in EU member states.
- For UK: ICO guidance on monitoring at work (March 2024) sets the bar.

### Proportionality
- The least-intrusive method that meets the legitimate purpose.
- Bulk surveillance for vague purposes is not proportionate.
- Time-limited; reviewed periodically.

### Access controls
- Access to employee monitoring data is restricted to a named, limited set of
  roles. Logs of access are kept and audited.

## Prohibited

- Covert monitoring (no notice, no consultation).
- Use of AI-summarised meetings as evidence in HR decisions without the
  affected employee being notified beforehand and given the source material.
- Using AI sentiment analysis as a primary driver of performance decisions.
- Profiling employees in protected categories (Equality Act 2010) for any
  decision that affects their employment.
